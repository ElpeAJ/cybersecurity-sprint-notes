# Day 2: Common Threats & The Threat Landscape

## The Five Threat Types
Understanding the primary attack vectors used by malicious actors to compromise organizational infrastructure:

* **Malware:** Malicious software engineered to disrupt, damage, or gain unauthorized access to a computer system.
* **Ransomware:** A specific sub-type of malware that encrypts a victim's files, with the attacker demanding a ransom payment to restore access. **(Primary CIA Target: Availability)**
* **Phishing:** Social engineering attacks where deceptive messages induce individuals to reveal sensitive personal or corporate data. **(Primary CIA Target: Confidentiality)**
* **Advanced Persistent Threats (APTs):** Highly sophisticated, prolonged, and targeted cyberattacks executed by well-funded actors (often nation-states) designed to remain slow and hidden for extended periods.
* **Insider Threats:** Security risks originating from within the organization, which can stem from any individual with authorized internal property access who behaves maliciously, carelessly, or fallibly.

| **Business Email Compromise (BEC)** | Phishing / Social Engineering | Manipulates employees into executing unauthorized financial transactions, causing major confidentiality breaches and financial losses. |

## Case Study & Activity Recap
Analysis of practical scenario classifications reviewed during today's session:

1. **City Government Ransom Demand:** Classified as **Ransomware** (Data locked down maliciously for financial extortion).
2. **Fake Bank Email:** Classified as **Phishing** (Deceptive communication designed to harvest login credentials).
3. **14 Months of Quiet Extraction:** Classified as an **Advanced Persistent Threat (APT)** (Sustained, stealthy data infiltration over an extended timeline).
4. **HR Text Message Scam:** Classified as **Smishing** (A variation of phishing executed entirely over SMS messaging channels).

> 💡 **Core Exam Takeaway:** Real-world cyber incidents frequently blur textbook categories together. For the upcoming Week 1 MCQ, focus on analyzing the operational details of what actually happened during the attack sequence rather than just relying on generic categorical labels.
