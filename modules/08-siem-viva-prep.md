# Day 8: SIEM Concepts & Alert Triage Operations

## SIEM Fundamentals & Logging Architecture
* **SIEM Definition:** Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) is a centralized software platform that automatically aggregates log data from across an organization's network, endpoints, and systems into a single searchable dashboard.
* **The Scaling Problem:** Without a centralized SIEM tool, a SOC analyst would have to manually connect to and audit logs on dozens of standalone systems separately, which is completely unscalable.
* **Alert Thresholds:** The fine-tuning parameters that determine when a rule triggers an alert:
  * *Threshold Set Too Low:* Analysts encounter **Alert Fatigue**, drowning in benign noise and potentially missing real threats buried in the queue.
  * *Threshold Set Too High:* True security incidents pass through unflagged and slip past outer monitoring controls unnoticed.

---

## The Four Core Triage Outcomes
Every alert generated by a SIEM platform must be triaged into one of these specific buckets:

1. **True Positive (TP):** The alert correctly identified a genuinely malicious, unauthorized, or dangerous event that requires immediate active response.
2. **False Positive (FP):** The alert triggered on an event that is actually legitimate, expected, or harmless corporate activity. No action is required, though the underlying SIEM rule may need fine-tuning.
3. **Requires Investigation:** There is insufficient data within the alert snippet to make a confident classification, requiring the analyst to pull additional system logs.
4. **Escalation:** A verified True Positive that is highly critical or sophisticated, requiring immediate handover to senior security engineers or the incident response team.

## Lab Worksheet: SIEM Alert Triage Solutions

Solutions and analytical justifications for the lab exercise packet alerts:

### Alert 1
* **Scenario:** 15 failed login attempts for user `jsmith` within 2 minutes, followed by a successful login from a new country.
* **Classification:** **True Positive**
* **Reasoning:** A dense block of 15 failed logins in under 2 minutes indicates a brute-force password guessing attempt, and the immediate successful login from a new country indicates the attack succeeded.

### Alert 4
* **Scenario:** A server's antivirus software flags and automatically quarantines a file matching a known malware signature, downloaded through a browser 40 minutes earlier. No further suspicious activity follows.
* **Classification:** **True Positive**
* **Reasoning:** The alert accurately caught a genuine malware signature threat; however, because the local system controls automatically quarantined the file successfully and no secondary lateral movement is seen, the incident is contained.

### Alert 5
* **Scenario:** A single failed login attempt occurs on an account, followed by a successful login 3 minutes later from the same device and same location as always.
* **Classification:** **False Positive**
* **Reasoning:** A single isolated failed login followed rapidly by a successful login from a baseline verified device indicates a normal human mistyped password event rather than an attack.

### Alert 6
* **Scenario:** A web server begins responding with an unusually high number of 500 error codes over a 10 minute window, coinciding with a spike in traffic from a single external IP address making rapid, repeated requests.
* **Classification:** **True Positive**
* **Reasoning:** The combination of a rapid external traffic surge from a single IP matched with server-side 500 crashes indicates an active Application Layer Denial of Service (DoS) or web exploitation attempt.

### Alert 7
* **Scenario:** An account with administrator privileges is used to access a file server at 3 AM. The account belongs to a systems administrator whose shift, according to internal scheduling records, runs from 11 PM to 7 AM.
* **Classification:** **False Positive**
* **Reasoning:** While a 3 AM administrative access looks highly suspicious out of context, checking the internal shift logs confirms the employee was actively on duty during their authorized working hours.

### Alert 8
* **Scenario:** A new, unrecognized device connects to the corporate Wi-Fi network using valid employee credentials, then immediately attempts to access the HR system's salary database, a system that account has never accessed before in its history.
* **Classification:** **Requires Investigation**
* **Reasoning:** The connection from an unmanaged device matched with highly anomalous, uncharacteristic access to sensitive corporate salary databases indicates potential credential theft, requiring immediate verification with the user.

### Alert 9
* **Scenario:** A marketing department laptop attempts to connect to a known command and control server address 6 times over 2 hours, each attempt lasting only a few seconds before failing.
* **Classification:** **True Positive**
* **Reasoning:** Repeated automated beaconing attempts to a known malicious Command and Control (C2) infrastructure address indicate the laptop has been compromised by malware attempting to phone home.

### Alert 10
* **Scenario:** A scheduled backup job, configured 6 months ago and running every night without incident, triggers a large outbound data transfer alert at 1 AM, matching its usual nightly pattern exactly.
* **Classification:** **False Positive**
* **Reasoning:** The massive outbound data shift aligns perfectly with a pre-configured baseline routine that has run securely for 6 months without any structural deviations.

## 🎓 Viva Voice Exam Preparation Script

### The Viva Prompt:
> *"Explain how to distinguish a true positive from a false positive using Alert 8 from your triage exercise."*

#### 🗣️ Strong Answer Formulation (Copy This Structure for the Interviewer):
"Alert 8 flags an unrecognized endpoint authenticating onto corporate Wi-Fi with valid staff credentials, which then instantly attempts to access an HR salary vault it has zero historical record of interacting with. 

I classified this alert as **Requires Investigation**. A weak response would blindly label this a True Positive just because the device is new. However, as an analyst, I must consider contextual business logic: this could be an authorized employee connecting a new personal phone to access their own payroll data. 

To definitively separate a true positive compromise from a false positive mistake, I would withhold guessing, check the DHCP logs to identify the device type, and cross-reference with the employee to confirm if they initiated the request."

## Real-World Impact: Why Triage Matters
* **Historical Failure:** In several major public corporate breaches, alerts detailing the initial hacker entries were sitting inside SIEM data logs weeks before the damage occurred. However, because analysts were overwhelmed by alert fatigue, the alerts were left unreviewed.
* **Defensive Practice:** Exceptional triage is often the single boundary lane separating early containment from an absolute disaster.
